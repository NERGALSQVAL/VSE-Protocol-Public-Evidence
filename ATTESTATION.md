1. Purpose

VSE Protocol is designed to transform authenticated renewable-energy production data into immutable, single-use, machine-verifiable digital energy proofs.

The protocol does not treat a manually entered value, spreadsheet, screenshot, user declaration, or self-reported production total as authoritative energy evidence.

The intended verification path is:

PHYSICAL ENERGY ASSET
        ↓
MANUFACTURER-AUTHORITATIVE DATA SOURCE
        ↓
VSE RAW EVIDENCE CAPTURE
        ↓
CRYPTOGRAPHIC HASH
        ↓
DETERMINISTIC VALIDATION
        ↓
ENERGY CALCULATION
        ↓
AUDIT CHAIN
        ↓
VSE UNIT
        ↓
MINT
        ↓
ALLOCATION / SALE
        ↓
RETIREMENT
        ↓
FINAL PROOF

2. What VSE Attests

For every valid VSE proof, the protocol is intended to attest that:

The underlying production evidence originated from an approved machine-authoritative source.

The raw source response was captured before proof creation.

A cryptographic fingerprint of the raw evidence was calculated by VSE.

The source identity and relevant device metadata passed protocol validation.

The production interval passed timing and duplicate checks.

The energy quantity was calculated according to the active VSE methodology.

The resulting verification state was linked into the VSE audit chain.

The same source evidence and production interval cannot be accepted repeatedly as new energy.

A VSE Unit cannot be treated as available after final retirement.

The final proof can be independently checked against the recorded cryptographic state.

3. Authoritative Source Requirement

VSE accepts only data associated with an identifiable physical energy asset and retrieved through an approved machine-authoritative path.

Examples include:

solar inverter telemetry;

smart-meter telemetry;

approved hardware telemetry systems;

official manufacturer infrastructure;

authenticated manufacturer cloud or OpenAPI access representing data generated by the underlying physical device.

The following are not accepted as primary production evidence:

manual UI entry;

spreadsheets;

user-uploaded CSV production totals;

screenshots;

human declarations;

manually edited payloads;

unverifiable third-party production totals.

If the source cannot satisfy the active VSE source-verification rules, the evidence is rejected.

4. Raw Evidence and Cryptographic Integrity

VSE follows an evidence-first model.

The protocol is designed so that source evidence is captured before a verified unit is created.

For accepted evidence, VSE calculates its own cryptographic fingerprint. Where additional cryptographic mechanisms are available, the protocol may also verify authenticated requests, signatures, HMAC values, or equivalent integrity controls.

A supplied hash is not trusted merely because an external party claims it is correct.

VSE independently calculates and validates the cryptographic state.

5. Deterministic Validation

The protocol is designed around deterministic validation.

The same valid source evidence, processed under the same methodology and protocol version, should produce the same verification result.

Validation may include:

source identity;

plant identifier;

inverter identifier;

logger identifier;

meter identifier;

source timestamps;

interval start and end;

interval ordering;

duplicate detection;

missing or invalid samples;

excessive sampling gaps;

future timestamps;

source-response integrity;

previous audit state;

methodology version.

A human operator must not be able to convert mathematically invalid evidence into valid evidence.

6. No Human Override of Failed Evidence

Administrative access does not grant production authority.

Administrators may manage operational functions, users, organizations, access control, contracts, billing, support, or review workflows.

Administrators must not be able to:

fabricate renewable-energy production;

manually approve failed source evidence as valid;

rewrite immutable audit history;

mint unsupported energy;

bypass duplicate protection;

restore a finally retired unit to active inventory.

Human review may exist for compliance, investigation, support, or enterprise assurance, but it must not rewrite the underlying machine evidence.

7. Audit Chain

Accepted verification events are linked into an append-only audit history.

The governing rule is:

NEW INFORMATION CREATES NEW STATE. IT DOES NOT REWRITE OLD STATE.

Corrections, investigations, exclusions, and security actions should therefore create new auditable events rather than silently modifying historical evidence.

8. Duplicate and Double-Use Protection

VSE is designed to prevent the same underlying renewable-energy production from being represented repeatedly as new verified energy.

Duplicate protection may operate across multiple dimensions, including:

source evidence hash;

source identity;

production interval;

audit identifier;

VSE Unit identifier;

blockchain registry state.

A previously accepted interval must not become new inventory simply because it is resubmitted through another user, organization, API key, request, or transaction.

9. VSE Unit Model

A VSE Unit is a verification instrument, not a general-purpose transferable cryptocurrency.

The intended registry model is:

only authorized VSE infrastructure may mint;

units remain under VSE-controlled registry ownership;

no unrestricted transfer mechanism;

no public secondary market;

no arbitrary approval mechanism;

duplicate source/audit/interval minting prohibited;

retirement allowed only once;

retired state is final.

The blockchain layer is used as part of the verification and finality architecture. It is not the source of the renewable-energy measurement itself.

10. Retirement and Finality

Retirement represents final use of a verified VSE Unit.

A finally retired unit must not:

return to available inventory;

be sold again;

be retired a second time;

be reactivated;

be silently reassigned to another beneficiary.

A retirement record may cryptographically bind the unit to information such as:

beneficiary;

purpose;

sale or allocation;

retirement transaction;

methodology;

protocol version.

11. Machine-Verifiable Proof

A VSE proof may expose or reference:

public VSE Unit identifier;

verified energy quantity;

previous audit hash;

methodology hash;

mint transaction;

VSE proof signature.

The human-readable proof page is a representation of the underlying verification state.

The cryptographic state is the evidence.

12. Anti-Fraud Principle

VSE follows the principle:

I AM THE MATH.

The meaning is operational, not rhetorical:

no commercial relationship may override failed cryptographic validation;

no administrator may manually approve invalid machine evidence;

no buyer may cause the same verified energy to be used twice;

no source may create verified energy that the active protocol cannot validate.

The protocol accepts or rejects evidence according to its validation rules.

13. What This Attestation Does Not Claim

VSE Protocol does not claim, solely by operation of this software, to be:

an official Guarantee of Origin (GO);

an EECS certificate;

a government-issued renewable-energy certificate;

a regulated electricity settlement system;

automatically accepted under every jurisdiction or corporate reporting framework;

automatically sufficient for Scope 2 reporting;

a substitute for legal, tax, accounting, regulatory, or external assurance requirements.

Acceptance of VSE proofs by buyers, auditors, regulators, reporting frameworks, or counterparties depends on the applicable legal, contractual, technical, and assurance requirements.
